# Meridian — Vision

> *"Chart your enchantments."* — the enchanting table, remade as a system you
> build, tune, and navigate.

## The promise

Meridian takes over one thing: enchanting. Vanilla's table is a single "power"
number and a slot machine — you ring it with bookshelves, spend levels, and
hope. Meridian replaces that with five independent stats you raise by building a
workshop of themed shelves, then layers a book-banking library, salvage and
experience tomes, anvil upgrades, and 133 original enchantments on top.
Enchanting stops being a corner you visit and becomes a progression you chase —
from your first stone shelf to a room that pushes an enchant to level 100.
Everything about your world outside the table is left alone.

## The arc of a world

In your first hours enchanting looks familiar: place a table, ring it with
bookshelves, take what it offers. The shift comes when you craft your first
themed shelf and watch one stat climb — the room around the table becomes a
build with a purpose. Through the mid game you range across the Nether, the deep
dark, and the End for the materials that raise each stat, upgrading shelves in
place and banking spare books into a library instead of hoarding chests of them.
Late game is mastery of the whole board: an End-tier wall that reaches Eterna 50,
a Library of Alexandria dispensing your best books on demand, and tomes that lift
a perfect enchantment off a worn tool onto a fresh one. A 26-step advancement
tree marks the path from first shelf to Eterna 50.

## The features, as you'll play them

### The five-stat enchanting table
- **What it is** — The table's single power value becomes five stats: Eterna
  (how high your enchant levels climb), Quanta (how far a roll can swing),
  Arcana (how often rare enchantments surface), Rectification (how much a bad
  swing is trimmed), and Clues (how many of a slot's enchantments the preview
  reveals, 0 to 3).
- **How you use it** — Surround the table with shelves as you always have; each
  feeds particular stats, and the table's screen shows the totals. Clues let you
  read what a slot will give before you spend on it.
- **What changes for you** — Enchanting becomes a dial you set, not a coin you
  flip. With Eterna maxed the table reaches level 100, where vanilla stops at 30.

### The shelves you build
- **What it is** — 25 themed shelves across a progression: starter wood and
  stone, Nether and ocean early tiers, infused mid-tier upgrades, deep-dark and
  sculk late tiers, three End-stone shelves — the only way to reach Eterna 50 —
  and utility shelves for extra Clues or Rectification, an enchantment blacklist,
  and treasure unlocks like Mending.
- **How you use it** — Higher shelves are crafted at the enchanting table
  itself, gated behind the stats you've already built, then placed within range
  like bookshelves.
- **What changes for you** — Building the shelf collection *is* the progression;
  there is no single "best table," only the room you've earned.

### Crafting at the table
- **What it is** — When your table's stats clear a recipe's thresholds, its
  third enchant slot becomes a crafting result — shelf upgrades, Infused Breath
  from Dragon's Breath, tome upgrades, budding amethyst, echo shards, and more.
- **How you use it** — Meet the stat requirement, then click the result slot and
  pay experience, exactly as you'd take an enchantment.
- **What changes for you** — Your enchanting room doubles as the bench that
  refines its own upgrades.

### The enchantment library
- **What it is** — A storage block that pools enchanted books into a
  per-enchantment bank, then hands them back for experience. The Basic Library
  holds enchantments up to level 16; the Library of Alexandria, up to level 31.
- **How you use it** — Drop enchanted books in — a hopper can feed it in bulk —
  and each adds points on an exponential curve. Withdraw the level you want from
  its menu for experience.
- **What changes for you** — Spare books become a savings account instead of
  clutter, but you can only pull out a level you've actually deposited: a stack
  of Sharpness I never adds up to a Sharpness V.

### Tomes — salvage and experience
- **What it is** — Salvage tomes move enchantments between items: the Scrap Tome
  pulls one at random and destroys the source, the Improved Scrap Tome pulls them
  all and destroys it, and the Extraction Tome pulls them all and leaves the
  source intact. A ladder of XP Tomes banks experience levels — 10, 30, or 50.
- **How you use it** — Salvage tomes go in the anvil beside the item you're
  stripping. XP Tomes fill on right-click, empty on shift-right-click, and craft
  in tiers at the table.
- **What changes for you** — A perfect enchantment is no longer trapped on a
  dying tool, and a hard-won stack of levels survives the trip into the deep dark.

### Anvil upgrades
- **What it is** — The Prismatic Web strips every curse from an item while
  keeping its enchantments; an iron block repairs a damaged anvil by a tier; and
  the Tempered Core makes a piece of gear unbreakable.
- **How you use it** — Combine the item with the web, the iron block, or the
  core at the anvil.
- **What changes for you** — Curses, anvil wear, and durability grinding each get
  an escape hatch — any of which a server owner can switch off to keep the grind.

### 133 original enchantments
- **What it is** — A roster of 133 enchantments spanning melee, ranged, tools,
  mobility, mounts, elytra, and shields — from Excavate's 3×3 mining to True
  Flight, Gravitas, and Final Gambit.
- **How you use it** — They roll at the table, drop as loot, and combine at the
  anvil like any enchantment, respecting their own exclusivity rules.
- **What changes for you** — The pool is deep enough to build a loadout around,
  not just a handful of universal upgrades.

## Staying in control

Meridian ships with sensible defaults and adds no HUD element — nothing new sits
on your screen. Shelf stats and library contents surface only when you look at a
block with Jade or WTHIT, or open a recipe browser. Every value tunes live with
`/meridian reload`, no restart. Curse-stripping, anvil repair, the Tempered Core,
table duplication, and the Everfeast ration can each be turned off, and the
Sunder, Seeker, and Harpoon enchantments leave other players alone unless a
server enables their PvP effects. Any enchantment, Meridian's or vanilla's, can
have its cap retuned or be disabled outright — which pulls it from the table,
loot, and tooltips without harming gear that already carries it — and every
shelf's stat contribution is retunable by server owners. A player who changes
nothing gets the whole progression, defaults intact.

## Better together

Meridian is one of four Concord mods, each overhauling a different vanilla
system, and it stands entirely on its own — with no siblings installed, nothing
is missing.

- **Tribulation** raises the danger as you range farther and deeper; Meridian is
  the power curve that keeps you in step with it. Never required.
- **Prosperity** makes every chest worth opening; the books and gear it turns up
  flow straight into your library and tomes. Never required.
- **Mercantile** gives villagers a real economy; the surplus your enchanting
  throws off becomes something to trade. Never required.

## What Meridian will never do

Meridian owns enchanting and stops there. It adds no new dimensions or biomes and
no persistent screen clutter. It will not overhaul mob difficulty or world
scaling (Tribulation's domain), rewrite villager trading (Mercantile's), or
replace loot tables and containers (Prosperity's) — a request that reaches into
those is handed to the sibling that owns it, or declined. It is never a required
dependency for another mod. And it stays deferential to vanilla: it completes the
enchanting table, it never declares it broken.
